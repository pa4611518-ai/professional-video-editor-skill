# Professional editorial craft

Use this guide to make editorial decisions, not to force a particular style. The footage, brief, audience, and platform determine the treatment.

## 1. Start with intent, not effects

Before cutting, summarize in one sentence what the viewer should understand, feel, or do. Identify the central subject, strongest evidence or moment, and any context that must remain for the message to be honest. Build the edit around that intent.

## 2. Structure and opening

- For short-form, promotional, or social content, establish the subject or viewer payoff early. Use a hook only when the footage supports a truthful, relevant hook; do not manufacture suspense or claims.
- For tutorials, demonstrations, and explainers, prioritize comprehension and show necessary steps in a logical order.
- For story-led material, preserve setup, cause-and-effect, reaction, and payoff. Do not remove context merely to make the edit faster.
- Make the ending intentional: finish the thought, land the emotional beat, show the requested call to action, or end cleanly. Avoid accidental mid-word or mid-action endings.

## 3. Cut decisions and rhythm

- Cut on completed thoughts, natural breaths, meaningful action changes, or shifts in visual information—not just at arbitrary intervals. Preserve the start and end of words that carry meaning.
- Remove a pause only if it does not communicate emotion, thought, tension, or natural pacing. Not every silence is dead air.
- Use reaction shots, inserts, or B-roll only if they exist in the source or are supplied/authorized and actually clarify or strengthen the sequence. Never invent coverage.
- Use J-cuts or L-cuts when overlapping audio improves continuity, anticipation, or emotional flow; do not use them as a default decoration. Adobe's documentation describes these as audio/video transitions where sound and image change at different moments.
- Vary shot scale and rhythm when the source supports it. Do not create fake variety through repeated digital zooms on the same frame.
- Match music beats only when the energy and message benefit; dialogue clarity and action continuity take priority over beat matching.

## 4. Continuity and visual attention

- Check screen direction, eyelines, action continuity, background changes, object positions, and matching movement when multiple shots are cut together.
- Use a cut, dissolve, wipe, speed change, or graphic transition only when its meaning and timing fit the content. A straight cut is the default when no transition is needed.
- Keep the intended subject readable. Reframe for the actual subject and action, not just the geometric center; preserve faces, hands, products, on-screen text, and relevant context.
- Avoid cutting at moments that make gestures, product use, or movement look broken unless that discontinuity is intentional.

## 5. Promotional/product video

- Make the product or service easy to identify quickly. Preserve the product's real appearance and do not alter its features, color, packaging, price, or claims.
- Sequence evidence before claims: show the real product/action or supplied proof before making a statement about it. Do not fabricate testimonials, results, features, scarcity, discounts, or guarantees.
- Keep calls to action visible long enough to read, but do not let them obscure the product or critical action. Use only CTA text supported by the brief.
- Choose energy based on the brand and platform. Premium, calm, funny, urgent, and tutorial styles have different pacing; do not equate professional with fast or flashy.

## 6. Captions and screen graphics

- Transcribe from verified audio when possible; do not rely blindly on automatic transcription. Double-check names, numbers, negations, prices, product details, and calls to action.
- Break captions into natural phrase units, avoid overly long lines, and keep them readable at mobile size. Avoid placing text over faces, hands, products, UI, or platform controls.
- Use a consistent type hierarchy, spacing, contrast, and placement. Animate captions only when motion improves comprehension or follows the brief.
- Captions should support speech, not rewrite its meaning. If editing spoken content changes context or implies a false statement, restore context or omit the cut.

## 7. A/B review questions

When two edit choices are plausible, compare short previews rather than debating abstractly. Ask:
1. Is the subject/message clear sooner?
2. Is context still accurate?
3. Does the rhythm feel intentional rather than rushed?
4. Is the important visual or spoken information easier to perceive?
5. Does the choice fit the brief and destination?
6. Does the simpler version communicate just as well? If yes, prefer the simpler version.
